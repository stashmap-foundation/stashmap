# Robin Dunbar, *Evolutionary Psychology.* (Oxford: Oneworld Publications, 2012). <!-- id:630f1645-5ae6-4f9a-b758-ccb331e96d55 -->

„While the group-level trait of shared religious beliefs enables religious groups to out-compete secular ones, this effect is modulated by the level of sacrifice involved. In religious communities, proof of one’s commitment is more convincing if the price of that proof is high.“ S. 23. <!-- id:1d3e201f-f00d-46be-88ef-ef4d15747a68 -->

„Adult humans have very good mind-reading skills: we readily infer and attribute mental states (like beliefs and desires) to other individuals, to explain their behaviour. Mind-reading of this kind leads to what Mameli terms ‘mind-shaping’, where the beliefs you hold about another person lead that person to act in a way that fits your beliefs about them.“ S. 28. <!-- id:0beee08c-a710-4c9d-aa01-5d7f4055312a -->

„Primate social systems, including those of humans, are implicit social contracts where, in effect, members agree to forgo their immediate self- interests, in order to gain greater benefits, in the long run, by solving some ecological problem more effectively.“ S. 111. <!-- id:93c45a91-525c-4485-9e5b-4d462de36f5b -->

„The social brain hypothesis suggests that the demands of living in permanent social groups selected for a kind of intelligence that was particularly adept at tracking the relationships that exist between oneself and all the other members of the group.“ S. 112f. <!-- id:8a7c407c-a279-44bc-a05f-976d109744e9 -->

„Relative neocortex volume correlates with various measures of social complexity across the primates. These indices [...] include such things as the size of the social group, the size of grooming cliques (or coalitions), the amount of social play, the use of tactical deception.“ S. 113. <!-- id:6ee1a0ab-4eb1-4268-9549-f4a7c2738ffd -->

„Apes have to use more computing power to maintain a group of a given size than monkeys do. In other words apes (and, hence, humans) must be doing something more complicated than monkeys to maintain the cohesion of their social groups, and this must have something to do with the complexity of their relationships rather than just with their number.“ S. 115f. <!-- id:af6515c8-12ed-4b07-b6bc-175d91448b95 -->

„The figure of approximately 150 seems to correspond to the number of people whose relationship to you is explicit and personal, with a history of past interactions and some level of intimacy. [...] Those who lie outside the circle of 150 we know only as categories of individuals.“ S. 119. <!-- id:76f13bc3-4fa7-4b18-ab34-4586a9953f07 -->

„Face-to-face contact seems to be crucial in maintaining the quality of the relationship at any given level; failure to maintain contact with someone will have the result of weakening the tie. [...] Time, it seems, is another constraint on the number of individuals we can fit into a given social circle.“ S. 120. <!-- id:f1a87a6a-6210-4dae-8224-3e9194b06fe0 -->

„The free-rider problem becomes increasingly intrusive under two general conditions: when social groups are large and dispersed and when the costs of co-operation are low. [...] free-riders will find it relatively easy to locate naïve individuals who are unaware of their behaviour.“ S. 126. <!-- id:e9a38884-169d-4d28-af8e-35d65a972bc9 -->

„Language was a prerequisite for evolving large groups because it provided the essential mechanism needed to weld them into coherent, stable communities of individuals. [...] language provides us with a mechanism for bonding large social groups through a form of ‘grooming at a distance’.“ S. 133f. <!-- id:2b601c37-26e9-466d-b13a-4f39ad32f5f0 -->

„Language was preceded by a phase that used some form of non-linguistic articulated speech for social bonding. The obvious candidate is music – specifically, wordless singing. [...] Music provides a bridge to language, because it seems to involve the same endorphin charge as grooming.“ S. 136f. <!-- id:2b7c2d62-ad30-4f72-b790-5c53ada7eb2f -->

„Institutional facts are facts about the world that exist only because we all agree that they do: marriage, government and money are obvious examples. [...] Searle argues that language and ToM [Theory of Mind] are crucial to the generation of institutional facts and therefore to culture as we know it.“ S. 144. <!-- id:9da4b5ce-05e6-48a2-a75e-e4be34475c9b -->

„Dual inheritance modelling has shown that a conformity bias can be a very powerful way of learning adaptive strategies in ‘information-poor’ environments, where it is difficult to obtain all the relevant facts by one’s own efforts. By adopting what the majority are doing, we average a great number of individual learning experiences and so arrive at a way of behaving that has been tried, tested and proven successful.“ S. 150f. <!-- id:c4044ea2-308c-40ed-ab7f-e687d3446d01 -->

„Religion (and, by extension, story-telling) plays a crucial role in creating a sense of community and bondedness. That effect acts for the benefit of the members, through a group-level effect, because the members of well-bonded groups have higher fitness than those of poorly bonded groups.“ S. 172. <!-- id:c601b950-7fb8-4cb7-9cba-a4a24b05461a -->

„People take their reputations very seriously and are often willing to defend them vigorously. This is because spreading the word about another individual’s behaviour is a powerful mechanism for controlling free-riders. [...] Gossip (in its negative sense) is one of the functions that language makes possible.“ S. 187f. <!-- id:7f3e1b63-a5de-4f42-a8bd-4e995176d315 -->

„A sense of ‘fairness’ which requires that miscreants receive their ‘just desserts’. [...] traits have been selected in humans by a process of multi- level selection at the group level. [...] people willingly repay gifts and punish individuals who violate fairness and co-operative norms.“ S. 190f. <!-- id:c2c3b126-7fa2-4fd4-80d3-f49dc5d7002c -->
